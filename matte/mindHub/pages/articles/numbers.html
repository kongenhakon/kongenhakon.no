Remembering Numbers

Why Are Numbers Hard to Remember?

Numbers can be difficult to remember because they often do not have a clear meaning or image.

A memory palace can make numbers easier to remember by connecting them to colors, objects, sizes, and locations.

Create an Association for 0–9

Start by giving every digit from 0 to 9 a permanent association.

You can use colors, objects, sounds, shapes, or anything else that is easy for you to imagine.

Number Color Example Object
0 White Ball
1 Black Stick
2 Red Shoe
3 Blue Car
4 Green Tree
5 Yellow Star
6 Orange Orange
7 Purple Hat
8 Brown Bear
9 Pink Balloon

These are only examples. You can create your own associations.

Use Size Too

Size can give you another way to distinguish between numbers.

1 could be a small black stick.

10 could be a huge black stick.

This allows you to create different images for related numbers.

Turn Numbers Into Objects

Instead of thinking about a number as a symbol, turn it into something you can visualize.

Instead of thinking:

3

Imagine a giant blue car.

Now your brain has a picture to connect with the number.

Put the Numbers in Your Memory Palace

Once you have created your number associations, place them inside your memory palace.

Imagine you need to remember:

1 - 5 - 3 - 8

You could imagine a black stick, a yellow star, a blue car, and a brown bear along your memory palace route. Or a small black ball for 0 and a big black elephant for 7. This way u can use a color multiple times.

Tip:

Write down your color for each number on paper so you remember what color is for what number.

Make the Images Extreme

Make your mental images strange, huge, funny, or completely impossible.

Tip:

A tiny normal object might be forgettable. A giant object destroying your room is much more memorable.

Remembering Long Numbers

Long numbers can be split into smaller groups.

Instead of:

5839217460

Think:

583 - 921 - 746 - 0

Each group can become a sequence of images placed along your memory palace route.

Keep Your Associations Consistent

One of the most important rules is consistency.

If 3 means blue for you, keep 3 as blue every time you use your system.

Changing the associations will make the system harder to use.

Practice

Start with the numbers 0–9 and practice your associations until they become automatic.

After that, start practicing two-digit numbers, then three-digit numbers, and eventually longer numbers.

Summary

  1. Create an association for every digit from 0 to 9.
  2. Use colors, objects, sizes, or other visual ideas.
  3. Keep the associations consistent.
  4. Turn numbers into visual images.
  5. Place the images inside your memory palace.
  6. Use a fixed route through the palace.
  7. Group long numbers into smaller sections.
  8. Make the images unusual and memorable.
  9. Practice until the associations become automatic.

With practice, numbers can stop looking like meaningless symbols and become memorable images inside your memory palace.
